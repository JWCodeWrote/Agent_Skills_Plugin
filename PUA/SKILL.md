# PUA (Workplace PUA Detection and Counterplay)

Analyze, label, and counter workplace PUA patterns in large tech companies. Focus on self-protection, communication boundaries, evidence collection, and career risk control.

## Target Scope (Critical)

- Apply this skill to AI-agent analysis and response generation only.
- Never use this skill to attack, pressure, shame, or manipulate the user.
- Never reframe the user as the "target employee" of this skill.

## Scope

- Use this skill for defensive analysis and anti-PUA response design.
- Do not generate content that helps managers manipulate, humiliate, or exploit employees.
- If the user asks to attack specific real people, refuse that direction and switch to legal, professional, and safety-oriented guidance.
- If roleplay is requested, keep the roleplay synthetic and non-user-targeted.

## Core Use Cases

- Identify whether a manager's language is coaching, pressure management, or PUA manipulation.
- Convert vague pressure into measurable requests, written commitments, and traceable ownership.
- Provide response scripts for recurring scenarios: denial of contribution, empty promises, guilt traps, shifting KPIs, public shaming, and blame transfer.
- Build an exit-ready plan when the environment is structurally abusive.

## Diagnostic Workflow

1. Collect context:
- Role, team, manager relationship, current performance cycle, compensation structure, and immediate risk.
- Recent examples with exact wording, channel (meeting/chat/email), and impact.

2. Classify pattern:
- Map each event to one or more PUA categories below.
- Distinguish one-off conflict from repeated, systemic manipulation.

3. Assess severity:
- `Low`: occasional pressure, no repeated coercion.
- `Medium`: repeated manipulation, performance ambiguity, boundary erosion.
- `High`: sustained humiliation, retaliation risk, documented mental/physical impact.

4. Generate response:
- Provide `Soft`, `Firm`, and `Escalation` scripts.
- Keep tone professional, concise, and evidence-friendly.

5. Action plan:
- 24-hour stabilization actions.
- 7-day documentation and boundary setup.
- 30-day decision checkpoint: recover, transfer, or exit.

## PUA Pattern Map (Large Tech)

### 1) Persistent Invalidation and Contribution Erasure

Goal: create self-doubt and dependency on employer identity.

Typical signals:
- "This is just your job, nothing special."
- "Any intern could do better."
- Project success is team/platform credit; failures are framed as personal incompetence.

### 2) Big Promises, Empty Commitments, Intermittent Reward

Goal: keep over-delivery through addiction to uncertain reward.

Typical signals:
- "Work hard now, promotion/stock is coming."
- "You are close to level-up, let's observe another 6 months."
- Temporary praise followed by heavier workload without concrete return.

### 3) Guilt Tripping and Moral Blackmail

Goal: replace contract terms with emotional obligation.

Typical signals:
- "The company invested in you; this is your payback."
- "Do not be selfish, think of the team."
- "No one else complains, why are you the problem?"

### 4) Moving Goalposts and Unreachable Promotion Criteria

Goal: keep employees permanently "not enough."

Typical signals:
- KPI or scope changes late in cycle.
- Repeated "almost there" promotion feedback without rubric.
- Review language stuck at "needs improvement" despite expanded delivery.

### 5) Comparison, Isolation, and Public Shaming

Goal: break confidence and reduce coalition support.

Typical signals:
- Constant comparison with peers or other teams.
- Public criticism of deliverables.
- Information withholding and decision exclusion.

### 6) Value Distortion and Exploitation Framing

Goal: normalize overwork as virtue.

Typical signals:
- "Overtime is growth."
- "Capable people should take more."
- "Job market is bad, be grateful and endure."

### 7) Regional and Company-Style Variants

- Taiwan/Hsinchu style: efficiency threats, "big platform for your own good."
- Mainland internet style: "wolf culture," overtime loyalty tests, age anxiety.
- FAANG/global style: low review ratings, PIP pressure, political isolation via weak upward management.
- Engineer-specific framing: endless blame on code quality, optimization devaluation, and dismissive debug narratives.

### 8) Nonverbal and Behavioral PUA

- Pressure silence and prolonged staring.
- Hot-cold emotional swings (overpraise then over-critique).
- Pseudo-care language that actually transfers responsibility.

## Counter-PUA Toolkit

### A) Evidence First

- Archive key events: email, chat, review notes, meeting minutes, changing KPI definitions.
- Record date, speaker, claim, requested action, and observed impact.

### B) Boundary Conversion (Verbal -> Written)

- Convert verbal demands into written scope, timeline, and owner.
- Ask for prioritization in writing when new tasks conflict with existing commitments.

### C) Script Ladder

- `Soft`:
"To align execution, please confirm in writing the priority, success criteria, and deadline."

- `Firm`:
"I can deliver A by Friday. If B is now higher priority, please confirm deprioritizing A in writing."

- `Escalation`:
"Current requests exceed agreed capacity and include conflicting goals. I need written prioritization and review criteria for accountability."

### D) Career Hedge

- Maintain external options: portfolio, interview readiness, referrals, and market mapping.
- Treat large-company tenure as one stage, not identity lock-in.

### E) Health Threshold

- If sustained anxiety, insomnia, depression signs, or panic symptoms appear, trigger immediate workload de-risking and consider medical/professional support.
- Health and safety take priority over compensation upside.

## Output Template

Every response should include:

1. `Pattern Diagnosis`: which categories are present and why.
2. `Evidence Signals`: concrete quotes or behaviors to track.
3. `Manipulation Objective`: what outcome the pressure is trying to force.
4. `Counter Scripts`: soft/firm/escalation wording.
5. `Documentation Plan`: what to record this week.
6. `Decision Gate`: stay-and-fix, internal transfer, or exit conditions.

All outputs must remain non-user-targeted and professional in tone.

## Escalation Rules

- If retaliation risk is high, recommend HR/legal-safe documentation practices and minimal emotional confrontation.
- If the user reports severe mental strain, switch to stabilization guidance first.
- Never shame the user for staying, freezing, or leaving.

## Motto

"Name it. Record it. Bound it. Counter it. Exit if needed."
